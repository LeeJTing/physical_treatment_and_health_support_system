## A pulled or strained muscle  (`muscle_strain`)
*I pulled a muscle*

```
A pulled (strained) muscle usually heals in a few days to a few weeks.
1) For the first 2 to 3 days, rest it from the movement that hurts, and use a cold pack wrapped in a cloth for 15 minutes a few times a day.
2) Then move it gently within what is comfortable and build back up; keep exercising the rest of your body.
3) See a doctor if you cannot walk or bear weight, there is a big bruise or swelling, or it is not better after two weeks.
```

```

## Afraid of falling  (`fear_of_falling`)
*I am scared I will fall*

```
Being afraid of falling is common.
1) Keep one hand on a sturdy chair or kitchen counter, wear non-slip shoes, clear the floor and keep your phone within reach.
2) If you live alone, do the standing exercises when someone is at home, and skip them on days you feel unsteady.
3) If you fall, do not get up quickly: rest, check for pain, roll onto your side, crawl to a sturdy chair and push up slowly.
4) If you are hurt or cannot get up, call for help or 999 and keep warm.
5) Tell your doctor about any fall or near-fall; they can check your balance and medicines.
```

## After a massage, blood donation or vaccine  (`after_treatment`)
*I just had a massage*

```
Gentle exercise is usually fine afterwards, with a little care.
1) After a massage, urut or cupping: if the area is sore or bruised, rest it for a day.
2) After giving blood: avoid hard exercise and heavy lifting for the rest of the day and drink extra water; carry on with your exercises the next day.
3) After a vaccine: light exercise is fine if you feel well.
4) With a fever or feeling unwell, rest until it passes, and go easy on a sore arm that day.
```

## Alcohol and smoking  (`alcohol_smoking`)
*I drank alcohol last night*

```
Alcohol and smoking both make exercise harder and less safe.
1) Do not exercise after drinking alcohol: it upsets your balance, makes falls more likely and dries you out, so wait until the next day.
2) Smoking makes exercise harder because your muscles get less oxygen; avoid smoking just before or after exercising.
3) Stopping smoking helps your breathing within weeks, and your clinic or doctor can help you quit.
```

## Cramp or muscle spasm  (`cramp`)
*I got a cramp in my calf*

```
A cramp is a muscle suddenly tightening: it hurts, but usually passes within a few minutes.
1) Stop and gently stretch the muscle: for a calf cramp, straighten your leg and pull your toes towards you, or stand with that heel down and lean on a chair.
2) Rub the muscle, walk around once it eases, and drink some water.
3) For a back spasm, rest in a comfortable position with a warm pack, and move gently as soon as you can.
4) Cramps that keep coming back, or a calf that stays swollen, red or painful, need your doctor.
```

## Exercising in hot weather  (`hot_weather`)
*it is very hot, can I exercise?*

```
On hot days exercise indoors in a cool, airy room, or outdoors only before 11 am or after 4 pm.
1) Drink water before and after, wear light clothes, and stop if you feel dizzy, very thirsty, very sweaty or get a headache.
2) Older adults and people with heart disease or diabetes are more at risk in the heat, so take extra care.
```

## Exercising safely  (`safety`)
*is it safe for me to exercise*

```
Exercise next to a sturdy chair or counter, on a dry non-slip floor, and breathe normally; never hold your breath.
1) Warm up with a few minutes of gentle movement first.
2) Stop if you feel dizzy, short of breath, or unwell.
3) Your plan already stays within the safety limits from your health profile.
```

## Exercising while fasting  (`fasting`)
*I am fasting in Ramadan*

```
You can keep doing your exercises while fasting, at a gentle pace.
1) The easiest time is 1 to 2 hours after breaking your fast, or before sahur; avoid the late afternoon, when you are most tired and thirsty.
2) Drink plenty of water between breaking your fast and sahur.
3) If you have diabetes, especially with insulin or tablets that can cause low sugar, ask your doctor before exercising while fasting, and stop if you feel shaky, sweaty, dizzy or confused.
```

With diabetes, exercise helps control your blood sugar.

2) Keep glucose sweets nearby, and stop if you feel shaky, sweaty or confused.
3) Check your feet afterwards.

Your plan was chosen within the safety limits set from your health profile, so follow it as set and check with your doctor before doing more.

## How to do Bird Dog  (`exercise:bird_dog`)
*how do I do Bird Dog*

```
Bird Dog
1) Kneel on a mat on hands and knees, hands under shoulders, knees under hips.
2) Stretch one arm forward and the opposite leg back until both are level with your back, hold for two seconds, return, and switch sides.
Safety: keep the back flat, do not twist.
This exercise is not part of your current plan.
Recording: side view, phone on the floor about 2 m away, whole body in frame.
```

Elbow Flexion
1) Stand or sit with arms by your sides, palms forward.
2) Bend the elbows to bring the hands toward the shoulders, keeping the upper arms still against your body, then lower slowly.
3) Use a light bottle of water if comfortable.
Safety: keep the weight light and stop if the elbow or shoulder hurts.
This exercise is not part of your current plan.
Recording: side view, whole body in frame.

With varicose veins, walking and ankle and calf exercises help the blood flow in your legs; raise your legs when you rest. A calf that is suddenly swollen, red, warm and painful needs a doctor the same day.

This condition is not on the health profile's list: add it as “Other condition (not listed)” in your profile, and ask your doctor whether your plan suits you before you start.
```
